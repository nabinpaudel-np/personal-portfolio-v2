## Operating mandate

Walked into Webpoint as a Project Management intern with no real authority and an honest curiosity about how software actually gets shipped. Over four years, I moved from assisting senior PMs to owning the full delivery pipeline and eventually leading the PM department.

The mandate was simple and brutally operational: stop the misses. The work spanned enterprise clients in the US, engineering squads in Ukraine, and a growing team in Nepal. Communication drift was the killer — every handoff lost fidelity, every sprint lost a day to timezone math, and every release carried a non-trivial chance of going sideways.

## Progression timeline

- **2021 — Intern.** Shadowed PMs on US client accounts. Wrote the first standardized scope template the company ever used.
- **2022 — Project Manager.** Owned full delivery for three enterprise accounts. Introduced a written async standup format that the company still runs today.
- **2023 — Senior PM.** Took over the highest-churn account. Cut churn to zero in two quarters by rebuilding the handoff protocol between sales and delivery.
- **2024 — PM Lead.** Structured the PM department. Hired and trained four PMs, wrote the SOP library, owned the pre-sales scoping process end-to-end.

## Execution breakdown

### 1. Standardized sprint ceremony

Replaced a calendar-driven standup with an async-first protocol. Standups only ran live when something was red. Sprint demos were mandatory. Carryover dropped to under 10% within a quarter.

### 2. Pre-sales scoping SOP

Built a structured scoping document that tied every estimate back to a written acceptance test. Sales stopped handing over "good vibes" briefs. Delivery started every project with a frozen backlog.

### 3. Release gating

Introduced a QA gate before any client-facing release. Engineering had to pass the acceptance test before code hit production. The number of hotfixes per quarter dropped by 80%.

## Outcome

- Zero client churn across the accounts I owned.
- Four PMs trained and placed into delivery roles.
- SOP library still in use across the company.
- Promoted into department leadership within three and a half years of starting as an intern.

The lesson that stayed with me: most operational problems are agreements that were never written down. Write them down, defend them, and the work gets boring — in the best possible way.
