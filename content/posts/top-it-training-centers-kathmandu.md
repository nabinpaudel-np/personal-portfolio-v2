The number of IT training institutes in Kathmandu has grown fast over the last five years. Some are excellent. Some are content mills. Picking the wrong one wastes six months and a non-trivial amount of money.

These are the five I keep recommending to people asking me where to start. I'm listing personal opinions, not endorsements — reach out if you want a listing changed or removed.

## IT Training Nepal

A Putalisadak-based institute focused on industry-oriented training. The faculty is mostly working professionals, which means the curriculum tracks what companies actually hire for rather than what looks good on a brochure.

**Location:** Putalisadak, Kathmandu

**Courses offered:**

- Python Programming
- Full Stack Web Development
- Machine Learning
- Spring Framework
- Ethical Hacking
- Cloud Computing

**Best for:** Students who already know what they want to specialize in and want a structured path to job-readiness.

## Trainingpoint

Founded and run by working IT professionals rather than education entrepreneurs. That distinction matters — the courses are designed by people who actually hire engineers, not by people who only teach. Project-based learning is the default.

**Location:** Gairidhara, Kathmandu

**Courses offered:**

- Project Management Training
- Web Development Training
- UI/UX Design Training
- Mobile Development Training
- Manual QA and Automation QA Training
- DevOps Training

**Best for:** Career switchers and new graduates who need both technical training and exposure to how software teams actually work. The PM and QA tracks are particularly strong.

## Broadway Infosys

One of the longer-running IT training brands in Kathmandu. The curriculum is broad — almost every mainstream IT track is covered. The differentiator is the personalized learning approach: students can shape their track to match career goals rather than following a fixed path.

**Location:** Tinkune, Kathmandu

**Courses offered:**

- PHP Training
- .NET Training
- Laravel Training
- iOS Training
- Flutter Framework Training
- Python Training
- MySQL / Oracle Training
- Core / Advanced Java Training
- Computer Basics & Microsoft Office Training
- Accounting Package Training
- Digital Marketing Training

**Best for:** Students who want breadth first and specialization later. Also a reasonable starting point if you're not sure which track fits you yet.

## Tech Axis

Focused on emerging technologies and innovation-oriented teaching. The curriculum updates frequently to track industry shifts — useful if you're targeting roles in newer stacks like MERN, React Native, or DevOps.

**Location:** Kumaripati, Lalitpur

**Courses offered:**

- Software Development
- MERN Stack Training
- React Native Training
- Machine Learning
- DevOps
- UI/UX Design

**Best for:** Students who want to enter the market on modern stacks rather than legacy ones.

## Laba IT Training Center

A smaller institute with a strong emphasis on foundational skills and a collaborative learning environment. Less of a "ship you into a job in three months" pressure and more of a "build the fundamentals properly" approach.

**Location:** Anamnagar, Kathmandu

**Courses offered:**

- Java Programming
- Network Administration
- Data Analysis
- Project Management

**Best for:** Students who want a solid foundation before specializing, or who are switching careers from a non-IT background.

## How to pick

A few questions that help narrow it down:

- **Do you already know which track you want?** Pick the institute strongest in that track. Don't pick based on brand.
- **Do you learn better through projects or lectures?** Project-heavy: Trainingpoint, Tech Axis. Lecture-heavy: IT Training Nepal, Broadway Infosys.
- **What's your budget and timeline?** All five are within a similar price band, but bootcamp-style intensive tracks cost more than part-time ones.
- **What do alumni actually do after graduating?** Ask the institute for placement data. If they can't show it, be skeptical.

## What I'd actually recommend

If I were starting over in Kathmandu today, I'd do this:

1. Pick **one** institute based on the specific track I wanted.
2. Spend the first month just learning the foundations — don't rush.
3. Start building a portfolio project in month two. The institute's coursework is the floor, not the ceiling.
4. Network on LinkedIn from day one. Connect with two or three people per week who are working in the role you want.

The institute gives you structure. The portfolio and the network get you hired.

## Closing

The IT training market in Kathmandu has matured. There are real options for almost every track, and the gap between the top institutes and the bottom ones is wide enough that picking carefully matters.

These are my five. Your experience may differ — and if you've had a particularly good or bad experience with any of them, I'd genuinely like to hear about it.
